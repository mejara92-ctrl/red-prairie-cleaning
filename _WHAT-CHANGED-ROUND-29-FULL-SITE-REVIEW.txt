RED PRAIRIE — ROUND 29: FULL-SITE REVIEW
Price-drift repair, /book funnel fixes, /call sales tooling, and the
site's first real internal-link structure.
========================================================================
Instruction: "Do a full review on my website, make any recommendations
and fixes that are good. /book needs to be a great sales funnel, /call
needs to help my assistant sell cleans."

Everything below was found by auditing the shipped files and driving the
live pages in a headless browser at 320/360/390/414/768/1280px. Fixes are
applied; the open recommendations are listed at the bottom.


========================================================================
A. PRICE DRIFT — the site was publishing four different wrong prices
========================================================================
rp-pricing-engine.js opens with a rule: prices live here, never duplicate
one into a page. That rule was being kept inside /book and /call and
nowhere else, and nothing checked. Live, simultaneously:

  index.html FAQ ................ carpet "$75 per carpeted room"   (engine: $50)
  carpet-cleaning-lawton-ok ..... "$75 per room", in BOTH the visible
                                  FAQ and the FAQPage JSON-LD schema    (engine: $50)
  house-cleaning-lawton-ok ...... "Deep cleaning starts at $199"    (engine: flat $300)
  house-cleaning-lawton-ok ...... "Maintenance cleaning starts at $149" (engine: $150)
  recurring-cleaning-lawton-ok .. "Maintenance visits start at $149"  (engine: $150,
                                  less 10-20% on a recurring plan)
  index.html Offer schema ....... Inspection Ready "299", Express "179"
                                  — two full pricing rounds out of date
  call/index.html ............... hourly "4-hour minimum" in TWO places
                                  (engine HOURLY_MIN_HOURS = 3)

The deep-cleaning one is the expensive one: a visitor read "starts at
$199," clicked through, and the estimator said $300. That is a 50% jump
at the exact moment trust is being decided, on a site whose entire
positioning is "the price you see is the price you pay."

All fixed against the engine's real numbers.

NEW: tools/check-prices.py
  Reads the live constants out of rp-pricing-engine.js and greps every
  published page for retired figures and for copy claims that have to
  track a constant ("N-hour minimum", "N-room minimum"). Exit 1 on drift.
  It is not a second copy of the price list, so it cannot itself go
  stale. Run it before shipping any round that touches pricing:

      python3 tools/check-prices.py

  It has already earned its keep — it caught the second, hidden copy of
  "4-hour minimum" in call/index.html's TITLES map that the manual pass
  missed.

B. /book — FUNNEL BUGS
========================================================================
B1. HEADER WORDMARK OVERLAPPED THE FAQ / CALL / TEXT BUTTONS
    .rp-head is a grid with `grid-template-columns: 42px minmax(0,1fr)
    auto`, and .rp-wordmark used `justify-self:center`, which sizes an
    element to max-content. With white-space:nowrap on both wordmark
    spans it overflowed the middle track in both directions and printed
    underneath the FAQ button. Measured: 12px of overlap at 320px, 5px
    at 390px (iPhone 14/15/16 standard width). Clean at 360 and 414,
    which is presumably why it survived this long.
    Fixed by clamping the wordmark to its track and letting the copy
    wrap. Verified clean at 320/360/390/414/768/1280.

B2. EVERY PRIMARY CTA RENDERED TWO ARROWS
    `.rp-primary::after { content: "→" }` supplies an arrow, and five
    buttons ALSO passed rpIcon("chev") inline — so "Continue › →",
    "Show My Price › →", "Looks good, continue › →", and both "Request
    a Callback › →". Removed the inline chevrons; the CSS arrow stays.

B3. THE FOUR EXPRESS ADD-ONS WERE UNREACHABLE AFTER PICKING A TIER
    The biggest revenue leak found. Round 25 moved fridge / oven /
    cabinets / Detail Pass onto the "moveouttiers" screen as checkboxes
    and filtered them OUT of the add-ons screen. So a customer who chose
    Express and then wanted the oven done had no way to buy it: the
    screen headed "Would you like any add-ons?" simply did not list it.
    The only path back was the browser Back button onto a screen framed
    as "pick a tier," about 3,300px of scrolling down on a phone.

    Those are four of the highest-margin SKUs in the catalog ($50/$50/
    $50/$150-240) on the tier most likely to want them.

    Round 25's actual concern was ORDER, not existence — prepending them
    ahead of carpet and windows pushed the bigger considered purchases
    behind "Show more." So they are back as their own labelled group
    BELOW the primary cards: carpet and windows keep the top slots, and
    nothing purchasable is unreachable. Same render path, same state
    fields, same line items, same webhook payload.

    The tier-switch guardrail came with them: stack all four on a 3BR
    and the screen now says "You're at $579 with these — Inspection
    Ready is $539 and covers all of it, guaranteed →". Verified live.

B4. HEADLINE PRICES SHOWED CENTS
    "$539.00" / "$249.00" on the price screen, the add-on chip and the
    quote card Liz reads aloud. Every price this business charges is a
    whole dollar. Added rpFormatMoneyDisplay() to the engine: whole
    dollars when the amount is whole, cents when it genuinely isn't (a
    10% military discount on $249 is $24.90 and still renders as such).
    The webhook / crew-sheet payload deliberately keeps .toFixed(2) —
    that's accounting data, not a headline.

B5. TOUCH TARGETS BELOW THE 44px MINIMUM
    /book's header FAQ / Call / Text buttons were 36px. That header
    exists to give a hesitating visitor a one-tap route to a human, and
    it had the smallest targets on the page. Now 44x44. Same floor
    applied across the landing pages (FAQ rows were 25px, the "Prefer
    to call / text" links 16px, footer phone links 16px) and to /call's
    Objections / End Call buttons (30px — tapped one-handed, mid-call).

C. /book — CONVERSION
========================================================================
C1. MILITARY DISCOUNT MOVED TO THE PRICE, AND MADE A REAL OFFER
    It was an unstyled bare checkbox at the very bottom of the invoice
    ledger, below every line item. In a Fort Sill town that is one of
    the strongest close levers on the page. It now sits directly under
    the price as a bordered offer card — "Military or first responder?
    Take 10% off." — in the same eyeful as the number it discounts.
    The itemised "−$25" line stays in the ledger where it belongs.

C2. HONEST SCARCITY AT THE PRICE MOMENT, NOT ONE SCREEN LATER
    The "Only N openings left in the next 7 days" line already existed
    and was already honest (it reads live calendar data and renders
    NOTHING when availability is healthy). It only appeared on the
    calendar screen — one step AFTER the decision that loses people.
    It now also renders on the estimate screen, under the same guard.

C3. SLOTS PREFETCH ON THE ESTIMATE SCREEN
    Availability used to be fetched when the calendar mounted, so the
    highest-drop-off screen in the funnel opened on a spinner. It now
    warms on the estimate screen — which is also what makes C2 possible.

D. /call — HELPING LIZ SELL
========================================================================
D1. A LINE SHE READS ALOUD WAS UNGRAMMATICAL
    quoteSayLine() built "So for a 3 bedrooms, 2 bathrooms, your total
    is..." — plural nouns behind the article "a". This is spoken
    verbatim to a paying customer at the single most important moment
    of the call. Now: "So for a 3-bedroom, 2-bath home, your total is
    $539 — ..."

D2. THE MILITARY DISCOUNT WAS PURELY REACTIVE
    The only place it appeared in the script was as an answer to "can
    you do better on price?" In Lawton that is backwards. Offered
    unprompted it costs at most $25 and buys a reason to say yes; held
    back until they complain, the same $25 only buys a save on a price
    objection you have already lost ground to. The add-ons step now
    scripts the ask, with the words, before the price is said.
    (Judgement call — reverse it by editing SCRIPT.addons.watch if you'd
    rather keep the discount in reserve.)

D3. A HARDCODED PRICE INSIDE AN OBJECTION NOTE
    The "how much for a move-out clean?" note hardcoded "$459" in the
    example quote — the exact thing the engine's own editing rules
    forbid, and the way every drift in section A started. Objection
    notes can now be functions (answers already could); this one reads
    RP_MOVEOUT_BEDROOM_TIERS[0].base live.

D4. THREE OBJECTIONS SHE HAD NO SCRIPT FOR
    Added, because these come up on real calls and were being improvised
    at the moments a booking is won or lost:
      "How do I pay? / Do you need a deposit?"  — "nothing due today"
        is the easiest true close on the list and wasn't written down
      "What if my move date changes?"  — the #1 reason a move-out caller
        says "let me call you back" instead of booking
      "I need it this week / my walkthrough is in two days."  — highest-
        intent caller on the board; must not be lost to a callback

D5. TWO JUMP-RAIL CHIPS SHOWED RAW STEP NAMES
    "intent" and "questionnaire" had no SHORT_LABELS entry, so the rail
    fell back to the internal identifier — two lowercase code names in a
    row of Title Case labels, on the one control she uses to navigate
    mid-call. Now "Reason" and "Checks".

D6. /call had no <h1> at all. Added.

E. SEO
========================================================================
E1. 16 OF 21 PAGES HAD ZERO INBOUND INTERNAL LINKS
    The single biggest SEO problem on the site. Every landing page
    linked only to "/" and "/book". The homepage linked to nothing but
    "/pricing". The footer's "Services" list and the on-page service-area
    cards were plain <li> and <h3> TEXT, not links. Which meant every
    city page, every service page, the resource center, the checklist
    and the deposit guide were reachable only from sitemap.xml — the
    weakest discovery and ranking signal there is.

    Added a crawlable link block to all 19 indexable pages: services,
    move-out-by-town, and the three free guides, with the current page
    rendered as non-link text (aria-current). Two variants, matching the
    two footer styles already in use.

    Inbound internal links per page: 0 → 18. Verified by crawl.

E2. THE HOMEPAGE HAD NO CANONICAL TAG
    Every other indexable page had one. The homepage — the page most
    exposed to ?utm_/?gclid/ /index.html / trailing-slash duplicates,
    and now the page carrying every one of those new internal links —
    did not. Added.

E3. NO PRICES ON THE LANDING PAGES
    These pages rank for cost-intent queries and published no number
    anywhere, so the visitor's actual question went unanswered until
    after they committed to the estimator. Added a price band to the
    seven move-out pages (both tiers, all four bedroom sizes, the
    military line, and a link to /pricing), mirroring the block the PCS
    page already had. Covered by tools/check-prices.py.

E4. STALE PRICES IN THE HOMEPAGE Offer SCHEMA — see section A.

E5. META DESCRIPTIONS OVER THE TRUNCATION LIMIT
    Homepage 251 chars, /pricing 281, the deposit guide 231. All three
    were comma-lists of services; rewritten to ~155 leading with the
    query intent and a real number.

E6. BROKEN HEADING OUTLINES
    Reviewer names were <h4> directly under an <h2> (16 pages), and the
    trust-bar badges were <h3> directly under the <h1> (17 pages).
    Neither is a document section. Now <p class="review-author"> and
    <b>, with the existing styling carried over — no visual change.

E7. SITEMAP HAD NO <lastmod>
    Added, plus priorities. Every URL just changed and wants recrawling.
    Keep the dates honest — a sitemap claiming everything changed today
    gets ignored.


========================================================================
F. RECOMMENDED, NOT DONE — needs your call
========================================================================
F1. THE HOMEPAGE → /book REDIRECT IS TECHNICALLY CLOAKING  ** read this **
    index.html redirects every human to /book but explicitly exempts
    crawlers by user-agent string. Serving different content to Googlebot
    than to users, keyed on the UA, is what Google's spam policy calls
    cloaking. The comment explains why it's done this way (a server 301
    to a noindex page would risk dropping the homepage) and that reasoning
    is sound — but the UA branch is the risky part, not the redirect.
    Options, roughly in order of safety:
      (a) Drop the redirect. Make the homepage do the selling and put a
          large estimator CTA above the fold — it mostly already does.
      (b) Keep /book as the landing page for PAID traffic only (it's
          already the ad destination) and let organic land on "/".
      (c) Make /book indexable with a canonical to "/", and redirect
          without the UA branch.
    I did not change this: it is a deliberate, documented business
    decision and reversing it would change where all your traffic lands.

F2. THE GUARANTEE BLOCK DOMINATES THE PRICE SCREEN
    On Express it is the largest element on the screen and sits directly
    under the number, and roughly half of it is "switch to Inspection
    Ready." Good upsell placement, but at the moment of price reveal the
    dominant message is "this tier might not be enough for you," which
    reads as doubt. Worth A/B testing a two-line version with the switch
    offer as a link only.

F3. "STEP 5 OF 10" IS A LOT OF STEPS TO SEE
    The progress counter is honest, but ten steps is a discouraging
    number at step five. Consider counting only the remaining decisions,
    or a bare progress bar with no numbers.

F4. DEAD CODE IN THE ENGINE
    rpDeepSqftTiers, rpMaintenanceSqftTiers, rpMaintenancePrice() and
    rpSqftTiersForService()'s deep/maintenance branches are unreachable —
    both services have been flat-anchor priced since round 12.
    SCRIPT.sqft and SCRIPT.condition in /call are likewise unreachable
    (no flow contains those steps). Harmless, but it's the kind of thing
    that makes a future reader think square footage still matters.

F5. THE LIGHTBOX <img src=""> ON 15 PAGES
    An empty src is invalid and makes some browsers re-request the page
    URL. JS sets the real src on open, so the attribute can just go.

F6. GOOGLE NO LONGER SHOWS SELF-SERVING REVIEW RICH RESULTS for
    LocalBusiness/Organization markup. Your AggregateRating and Review
    blocks aren't harmful and the reviews look genuine, so I left them —
    just don't expect stars in the SERP from them.
