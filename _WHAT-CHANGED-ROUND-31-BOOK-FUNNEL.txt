0. THE 5% QUESTION — what the numbers said
========================================================================
Re-derived from the worksheet formula the engine documents
(3 crew x $17.50 x 1.12 burden, + $15 supplies + $25 overhead,
/0.92 guarantee reserve, /0.80 target margin):

  tier    hrs     true cost    live price   margin$   margin%   $/crew-hr
  1-2BR     5       $363.04          $459    $95.96     20.9%      $30.60
  3BR       6       $426.96          $539   $112.04     20.8%      $29.94
  4BR       7       $490.87          $619   $128.13     20.7%      $29.48
  5+BR      8       $554.78          $699   $144.22     20.6%      $29.12

A 5% cut (a flat $30 off every tier) would have landed at $429/$509/
$589/$669 — margin 15.4% to 17.1%, reserve still funded. The problem is
what it does to gross profit per job:

  1-2BR   contribution $96 -> $66    needs +45% more jobs to break even
  3BR     contribution $112 -> $82   needs +37% more jobs
  4BR     contribution $128 -> $98   needs +31% more jobs
  5+BR    contribution $144 -> $114  needs +26% more jobs

A 5% price cut very rarely produces a 26-45% volume increase. Everything
in this round is the other lever: making the price easier to say yes to
without lowering it.

One thing worth keeping in view for later. Over rounds 25 and 26 the
1-2 bedroom tier went $299 -> $379 -> $459. That is +53% in two rounds,
on the most price-sensitive segment, and it is also the "from" price you
advertise. If resistance is real, that specific tier is where I'd look
first — cutting it alone costs almost nothing on the tiers where the
revenue actually sits. The a-la-carte guardrail holds either way (checked:
buying every Express add-on still costs more than switching tiers, with a
larger cushion at lower Inspection Ready prices, not a smaller one).

1. THE TIER SCREEN — the decision, and it was 2.9 screens tall
========================================================================
This is the screen where the customer picks a tier, so it is the most
important screen in the funnel. Measured at 390px it was 2.91 viewports
of scrolling and 285 words, and the two cards were 381px each — meaning
on a normal phone THE TWO PRICES COULD NOT BE SEEN AT THE SAME TIME. On a
screen whose entire job is "compare these and choose", that is the one
thing that must not happen.

  a) Cut the four highlight bullets from each card, and Express's
     exclusion paragraph. All of it repeated, in prose, what the scope
     table two inches below says in a grid. Cards are now the decision
     (price, crew, hours, guarantee); the table is the detail.
     Cards went 381px -> 281px and 379px -> 235px. Both prices now fit
     one screen.

  b) Removed the four Express add-on checkboxes that sat at the bottom.
     Round 25 put them there because round 25 had also filtered them OFF
     the add-ons screen, leaving them unreachable — round 29 fixed that
     and put them back where a purchase belongs, with a live "you'd be
     better off switching tiers" nudge attached. The copy here was a
     second set, and it asked the customer to buy Express add-ons before
     they had chosen Express.

  c) ADDED: what the price buys, on both cards.
     "$539 · 5-8 hours on site · That's 15-24 hours of work"
     "$249 · 3-5 hours on site · That's 6-10 hours of work"
     This is the strongest honest answer to "why does it cost that" and
     it was nowhere in the funnel. It also makes the tier gap argue for
     itself: Inspection Ready is 2.2x the price for about 2.5x the work.
     Computed from the crew size and hour range the card already shows,
     so it can't drift.

  d) ADDED: one real move-out review under the comparison. The screen's
     only social proof was a generic "5.0 / 61+ reviews" strip. A
     specific sentence from a specific move-out customer does more work
     than an aggregate at the moment of choosing.

  Result: 285 words -> 202, 2.91 screens -> 2.13, and the decision itself
  is above the fold.

2. THE ESTIMATE SCREEN — the money moment
========================================================================
  a) ADDED, directly under the number:
     "3 cleaners on site for 5-8 hours — 15-24 hours of work"
     $539 reads like an expensive cleaning. $539 for 15-24 hours of
     crew time reads like a rate. Same number, different question
     answered.

  b) ADDED, directly above the Book button:
     "No deposit. Nothing due today. You pay after the clean is done and
     you've seen it."
     This is already the easiest true close in Liz's phone script and it
     appeared NOWHERE on the website. Until you say otherwise, "book"
     reads as "hand over money now."

  c) ADDED: "Not ready? Text me this price instead."
     The screen had exactly one door: book now. Someone who likes the
     number but wants to check with their spouse or confirm their
     move-out date had nothing to press, so they left and the quote left
     with them. This posts the same partial lead the contact gate posts,
     to the same endpoint, flagged "Asked us to text the quote - not
     booked yet" so the office can see it's a warm quote waiting on a
     decision rather than a cold bounce. Fire-and-forget with keepalive,
     guarded against double-taps, and it can never block the screen.

  d) REMOVED from the Express guarantee block: "Need it guaranteed to
     pass a landlord or PM inspection? Switch to Inspection Ready before
     you book." That is word for word what the switch link immediately
     below it already offers. Two copies of the same upsell, stacked,
     directly under the price, made the largest block on the screen a
     paragraph about why the thing they just chose might be wrong.

3. TEN STEPS BECAME NINE
========================================================================
"Bedrooms" and "bathrooms" were two screens asking the same question
("how big is it"), each one tap. Merged into one "size" screen with both
rows rendered as compact pills instead of nine stacked full-width buttons.
Still two taps — whichever is tapped second advances, in either order —
but one screen transition instead of two, and it fits above the fold.

Removed the pre-fill where picking a bedroom count silently filled in the
bathroom count from the tier table. On a merged screen a pre-filled answer
looks like an answer the customer gave, and it would have fired the
auto-advance off a single tap.

Sessions saved mid-funnel under the old step names restart cleanly rather
than resuming onto a screen that no longer exists — verified.

4. THE STEP COUNTER IS GONE
========================================================================
"Step 2 of 9" at the top of a booking funnel is a countdown of work still
to do, printed above the work still to do. The progress bar under the
header already carries the same information without naming a number to
dread, and it was already on every screen. The bar stays; the number goes.
rpStepLabel() is kept (not deleted) because the analytics events still
want a human-readable label for each step.
