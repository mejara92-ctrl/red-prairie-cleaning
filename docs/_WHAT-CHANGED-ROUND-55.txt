RED PRAIRIE CLEANING — ROUND 55
FINAL PASS ON /book AND /call
=========================================================================

Instruction: analyse both surfaces fresh and make common-sense changes to
flow, wording and design.

PRICES UNCHANGED. Nothing here touches a number.

METHOD: walked every screen of both surfaces in a real browser at phone
and desktop width, exercised the states that don't come up in normal
clicking (empty and partial form submits, blocked jobs, calendar loading
and error, back navigation from every step), and measured every tap
target against the 44px minimum.

WHAT WAS ALREADY RIGHT, checked rather than assumed: contact-gate
validation (rejects a one-letter name and a short phone, accepts a
first name only and a formatted number), lead-form validation (names the
first bad field and scrolls to it), the calendar's loading and error
states, back navigation on every step, and the blocked-job path on both
surfaces. None of it needed changing.


THE FOUR REAL FINDINGS
----------------------

1. THE ADD-ONS SCREEN HID EVERY PRICE.  ← the big one
   Carpet, exterior windows and the garage floor each showed a title, a
   description and a chevron. No number. The customer had to tap into
   each card to find out what it cost.

   This is the one screen in the funnel whose entire job is selling more,
   and it was the only screen not leading with a number — the service
   list, the contact gate and the estimate all do. Nobody taps an
   unpriced item on a booking page; they assume it's expensive and scroll
   past.

   /call has shown these prices all along ("CARPET CLEANING — +$50/room"),
   so the two surfaces were also describing the same add-ons differently.
   Prices now render on the cards, read from the catalog rather than
   typed in — the round-29 review found $100/$200/$150 hardcoded into
   this file once already.

2. THE SERVICE PICKER HAD BECOME A WALL OF TEXT.
   Round 54 made every description carry its own "at $40/hr", which was
   accurate and turned each card into four or five wrapped lines. Only
   three of five services fit above the fold, and the featured Move-Out
   card was the most cramped of all — its wide "From $199" pill squeezed
   the title into two lines and the description into a four-line column.

   Every description is one line now, the featured card's pill matches
   the others ($199 / and up), and the shared rate is stated once beneath
   the list instead of three times inside it:

     "Deep, Basic and Hourly are all $40 an hour with one cleaner —
      you're choosing how many hours. Move-Out is priced for the whole
      home instead."

   One fact, one sentence, explaining all three at once. All five
   services now fit on one screen.

3. TAP TARGETS UNDER 44px.
   The questionnaire's Yes/No pills measured 41px and "Text me this quote
   instead" measured 28px. The pills are where a whole quote gets decided
   and they sit in pairs, so a mis-tap picks the opposite answer; the text
   link sits directly under the primary CTA, where a mis-tap books a
   cleaning nobody meant to book. Both are over 44px now. The
   questionnaire went from ten failing targets to two — and those two are
   the fixed header chrome at 42px, which is close enough to leave alone.

4. /call's QUOTE SCRIPT DIDN'T CARRY THE CONDITION CAVEAT.
   Round 54 put the heavy-condition disclosure in a coaching note on the
   home step. But the quote is the moment a caller commits a figure to
   memory — if the only time they hear "this covers a standard clean" is
   two screens earlier while they were describing their kitchen, they did
   not hear it, and a crew asking for more on arrival is the first they
   knew of it.

   When the caller has said the home is heavy, the spoken quote line now
   carries the caveat in the same breath as the number, and the coach
   note tells Liz not to skip it. That is the difference between a
   disclosed term and an ambush.
