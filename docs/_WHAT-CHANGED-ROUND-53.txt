RED PRAIRIE CLEANING — ROUND 53
THE ESTIMATE SCREEN, REBUILT
=========================================================================

Instruction, looking at the live screen: "the button is weird, there's
too much text, different fonts, make it simple."

PRICES ARE UNCHANGED. Move-Out $199 / $299 / $399 / $499, Basic $120,
Deep $240, Hourly and Extra Time $40/hr. This round is the screen only.


THE WEIRD BUTTON
----------------
"Not ready? Send this quote to my phone" was styled as a full-width 46px
box with a 1.5px grey border and muted grey text on a cream card. That is
not what a button looks like on this site — it is what a TEXT INPUT looks
like. Sitting directly under a heavy gradient CTA, the pair read as "big
red button, then an empty form field."

It is a plain underlined link now, which is what a secondary action
should be. Its confirmed state is a line of text rather than a filled
green box, for the same reason.


TOO MUCH TEXT
-------------
The screen carried eleven separate pieces of copy, and several said the
same thing twice:

  - "Base Service $399" sat under a $399 headline
  - "Estimated Time 8 hours" sat under "2 cleaners on site for 8 hours"
  - Bedrooms and Bathrooms rows repeated what the person had answered
    two screens earlier
  - the guarantee ran five sentences

Those four duplicated blocks are one line now:

    4 bed · 2 bath · 2 cleaners · 8 hours

and the guarantee is one sentence. The deposit comparison survived,
compressed from three sentences to one, because it reframes the price
rather than defending it — but it moved inside the guarantee panel
instead of floating under it as a second block.

The price breakdown only renders when there is something to break down.
With no add-ons the headline price is the whole story, and a one-row
"breakdown" of it is noise.


DIFFERENT FONTS
---------------
Not a bug. `button { font: inherit }` is set, the family is Manrope
throughout, and DM Serif Display is on the price only. It read as
inconsistent because the screen used about nine distinct type styles and
three separately tinted boxes — red guarantee, green payment panel, grey
detail rows — stacked down one column.

Now: three type sizes, one accent colour, one tinted panel. The serif
appears exactly once, on the number. Every additional serif would cost
that number some of its weight. The green payment box became a plain
sentence, which removes the third colour from the screen.


WHAT THIS SCREEN IS FOR
-----------------------
The rule the rebuild follows, worth writing down because it is what
stops the eleven blocks growing back: this screen confirms the price and
books the job. It is not the place to re-sell the service — that
argument was already won on the way here. Anything that is not the
number, what the number buys, or the way to proceed was cut.
