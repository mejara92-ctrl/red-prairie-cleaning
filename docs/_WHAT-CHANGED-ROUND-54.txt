RED PRAIRIE CLEANING — ROUND 54
/book: WHAT EACH SERVICE ACTUALLY IS
=========================================================================

Three instructions, all about expectation-setting:

  "This is an inspection-ready clean! Includes the full interior cleaning
   of the home designed for inspections."
  "Make sure customers understand deep or basic is just hourly."
  "Make them understand if the house is non-standard or has heavy trash,
   grime, an additional fee may be applied upon arrival."

PRICES ARE UNCHANGED. $199 / $299 / $399 / $499, Basic $120, Deep $240,
$40/hr. Nothing in this round touches a number.


1. THE MOVE-OUT IS INSPECTION-READY, AND SAYS SO
------------------------------------------------
Round 52 collapsed the two tiers and kept the name "Move-Out Cleaning",
which is what people search for. What got lost was the WHY — the old
tier was literally called Inspection Ready and the name did the
explaining by itself. "Everything inside the home" describes the scope
accurately and says nothing about what it is FOR.

The purpose is back without reviving the tier:

  service list   "Inspection-ready. The full interior, cleaned to pass a
                  walkthrough."
  scope screen   "Inspection-ready, inside and out" + the full interior
                  described against the standard a landlord checks
  estimate       one line under the price saying the same thing

One product, one price. It is now described by the standard it is built
to meet.


2. DEEP AND BASIC ARE HOURS, NOT OUTCOMES
-----------------------------------------
They always were — Deep is six hours at $40 and Basic is three at the
same rate, the identical rate Hourly charges. But the copy sold them as
outcomes: "a detailed, top-to-bottom clean of every room", "keep an
already-tidy home fresh". That is a promise of COMPLETION, and three
hours cannot finish a house that needs more than three hours.

That gap is where bad reviews come from. The customer was told "top to
bottom" and got three hours, and both of those are true.

Now, on the service list and on the screen before the price:

  Deep     "6 hours of cleaning at $40/hr."
  Basic    "3 hours of cleaning at $40/hr."
  Hourly   "Same $40/hr as Deep and Basic — you pick the hours."
  both     "It's time, not a finished checklist."

The screen heading changed too. "Here's what your Deep Cleaning
includes" invited exactly the checklist reading the body was trying to
correct; it reads "Here's what you're booking" on the three timed
services. Move-out keeps a scope heading, because move-out genuinely is
a fixed scope.

This is not selling it smaller. Six hours of a professional working the
list you care about is a good product — it just has to be the product
the customer thinks they bought.


3. THE ARRIVAL SURCHARGE, DISCLOSED BEFORE IT CAN HAPPEN
--------------------------------------------------------
A FOURTH QUESTION on the move-out questionnaire:

    "Is the home free of heavy trash and built-up grime?"

Round 24 cut this screen from five questions to three, so adding one
back needs a reason. It is this: the surcharge applies ON ARRIVAL, which
is the worst possible moment to introduce a number. A crew in a driveway
asking for more money is an argument, a refund request and a review, and
it happens precisely when nobody warned the customer it could.

One tap buys three things — the customer is told the policy at the
moment it could apply to them, they self-declare so any surcharge is
something they already agreed to rather than something sprung on them,
and the office is warned before dispatch instead of getting a call from
the driveway.

IT DOES NOT BLOCK AND IT DOES NOT PRICE. Unlike water/AC/mold, a dirty
house is a job we want — it just may cost more than the ladder says.
Answering "no, it's heavy" changes nothing about the quote. It shows a
note on the spot, flags the booking, and repeats on the estimate.

THE CONTRADICTION THIS CREATED, AND THE FIX. The site said, in four
places, "the price you see is the price you pay — no upcharges at the
door." That cannot coexist with an arrival surcharge, and the customer
would have been right to be angry about it. The promise is now scoped
everywhere it appears:

    "Standard home in standard condition — the price you see is the
     price you pay."

plus, on the estimate: heavy trash, heavy grime or anything outside a
normal clean "gets priced with you before the crew starts, never added
afterward." That is a smaller claim and a much more defensible one — and
still stronger than a competitor who won't publish a number at all.

Changed on: the homepage promise strip, /pricing's promise list, meta
description and worked example, and the estimate fine print. All of them
read from RP_MSG.price so they cannot drift apart again.


4. UX FIXES FOUND WHILE WALKING THE FUNNEL
------------------------------------------
THE CONTACT GATE contradicted itself. It printed the full price —
"Your 4-bedroom move-out comes to $399" — and then said "Name and
number, and it's yours." The thing being offered in exchange had already
been handed over two lines above, which reads as a bait and invites a
fake number. It asks "And where should we send it?" now, which is the
actual job of that screen.

THE QUESTIONNAIRE SUBHEADING said "Doesn't affect your price." Three of
the four answers still don't — but the condition question can, and
saying otherwise two taps before asking it would be exactly the bait the
surcharge gets accused of being. It reads "So the crew arrives ready for
the job, with the right number."

SERVICE LIST now shows hours alongside the price on Deep and Basic
($240 / 6 hrs), so the hourly rate is visible without arithmetic.


5. THE PHONE MATCHES THE SITE
-----------------------------
/call asks the same condition question, and Liz has the words for it on
the home screen plus a scripted answer for "so the price could go up
when you get here?" — which does not dodge and does not over-promise.
Her price lookup describes Deep and Basic as hours at $40 rather than as
flat packages. The crew sheet and the webhook carry the condition flag
(moveout_heavy_condition) so dispatch sees it before the van leaves.
