ROUND 61 — GETTING OUT OF THINGS, AND FEWER WORDS
===============================================================================
Brief: "It needs to be flexible and easy. Right now it's too many words, when
you hit the wrong window you get sucked into stuff and the back button doesn't
always work. Dumb it down make it simpler."

Three complaints. The middle two turned out to be one bug.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. "SUCKED INTO STUFF" + "BACK DOESN'T ALWAYS WORK" — THE SAME BUG
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THIS CONSOLE HAD FIVE FULL-SCREEN OVERLAYS AND ZERO HISTORY HANDLING. Not
some. None. Look up, Explain, Stuck, Steps, End all covered the screen
completely, and on a phone the way out of a full-screen thing is the system
back gesture. Pressing it did not close the sheet — it navigated off the
console entirely, mid-call. The only exit was a small Close button in the
top-right corner, the hardest spot on a phone for a thumb, and missing it
cost the call.

Worse, the most-opened sheet of the five was the worst offender: openLookup()
did not go through openSheet() at all, so it had its own private open path
that no fix would have covered by accident.

AND THE DOCK'S BACK WAS POSITIONAL, NOT HISTORICAL:

    function back(){
      const keys = sectionKeys(), i = keys.indexOf(rpState.step);
      if(i > 0) focusSection(keys[i-1]);
    }

It went one place up the LIST, never to where she actually came from. Jump
from the step sheet to the calendar, press Back, and you land on the address
— which is not where you were. At the first step it did nothing AND hid
itself. "Doesn't always work" is exactly right; it worked in one case out of
three.

FIXED:
  - Opening any sheet pushes a history entry. The system back gesture, the
    hardware button and the iOS edge-swipe now close the overlay like they
    close anything else, and stay on the console.
  - openLookup() routed through openSheet() so it can never drift again.
  - Back now means ONE thing — undo the last move — in this order:
        1. a sheet is open      -> close it
        2. she came from somewhere -> go back there (a real nav stack)
        3. otherwise            -> step back through the flow
  - Back is never hidden. At the true start it greys out instead of
    vanishing, because a button that disappears is one she stops trusting.

VERIFIED:
    Help / Steps / End open -> system back closes the sheet, stays on console
    dock Back with a sheet open -> closes the sheet, step unchanged
    quote -> jump to calendar -> Back lands on QUOTE (not contact)
    trail service>quote>home>calendar, four Backs: home, quote, service, service
    first step: visible, disabled.  after moving: visible, enabled.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. FEWER DOORS — FOUR BECOME TWO
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The dock offered Steps, Look up, Stuck? and Explain: four buttons opening
four full-screen overlays, three of which answer the same question — "I need
to know something." Look up already searches the whole corpus; Explain is one
entry inside it; Stuck is a fixed page she needs twice ever, hidden behind a
button she has to remember exists at the moment she is least able to remember
anything.

    was:  [ Steps ] [ Look up ] [ Stuck? ] [ Explain ]
    now:  [ Steps ] [ Help ]

The panic script is pinned to the top of Help, above the search results,
where someone who is stuck will actually hit it:

    Don't know the answer?
    "Let me check that properly and call you right back — what's the best
     number for you?"
    Never guess a price. Never promise a deposit back. Never push past a red box.

Every sheet also gained a full-width exit pinned to the bottom, where the
thumb already is, instead of only a corner Close.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. FEWER WORDS
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Measured per step on the phone, before -> after:

    service   107 ->  86        addons    120 -> 107
    caller     86 ->  69        quote     141 ->  92
    home      151 -> 127        contact    67 ->  51
                                calendar   57 ->  39

THE DO LINE NOW FOLDS. It ran 17-28 words on every screen:

    "Say the number once, plainly, then stop talking. Count to three in your
     head before you say anything else."

Good coaching, and the right thing to show someone on their first shift. By
the second week it is furniture between her and the controls, on every screen
of every call. Closed by default on the phone, one tap to open, and THE
CHOICE STICKS — it lives in localStorage because it is how this person likes
the tool, not a fact about this caller. A new CSR opens it once and it stays
open for her; Liz closes it once and it stays closed. That is the "flexible"
half of the ask. The desktop board still always shows it: the column is there
and has nothing to gain by hiding it.

THE QUOTE STEP PRINTED THREE CLOSING LINES, every time. Three alternatives is
not help — it is a decision, handed to her in the two seconds after she has
said a price out loud and is supposed to be silent. She says one of them. Now
one shows in full and the other two sit behind "Other ways to say it".

The SAY block was left alone at 16-36 words. It is the sentence she reads out
loud and the entire reason the tool exists.
