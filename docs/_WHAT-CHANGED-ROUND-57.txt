ROUND 57 — THE ANALYTICAL PASS
===============================================================================
Brief: "Check it from an analytical perspective. Make sure what we're saying
makes sense, and in the right order, and it's not redundant. As long as the
customer knows they're getting an inspection ready move-out, and that if the
move-out is heavy duty then we might say it's gonna cost extra. If it's
flagged just call us. Make it make sense. Make sure the theme is consistent
throughout as far as style."

No pricing changed this round. $199 / $299 / $399 / $499 and $40/hr are
untouched, and check-prices.py confirms no drift across 20 published pages.
This round is about what the words say, the order they say it in, and whether
the funnel draws the same idea the same way twice.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. THE CONDITION MESSAGE WAS SAID THREE TIMES ON ONE PATH
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A customer who ticked "no, the home is heavy" used to see the same idea in
three places:

  - the questionnaire note, right after answering
  - the flagged note on the estimate
  - the fine print at the bottom of the estimate

The last two sit on the same screen, four inches apart, saying nearly the same
sentence. Said twice on one screen it stops being emphasis and starts being
boilerplate — and the second telling makes the first read as legal cover
rather than a promise.

FIX: the fine print is now conditional. When the home is flagged, the note
carries the message, with the specifics and a phone number, and the fine print
drops out. When the home is standard, the note does not render at all, so the
fine print is the only place the exception is stated and it stays — that
customer has not seen it anywhere else in the funnel. Exactly one of the two
renders, ever.

Verified: flagged → condition panel present, fine print absent. Standard →
condition panel absent, fine print present.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. THE CLAUSE THAT WAS MISSING FROM EVERY VERSION OF THE CAVEAT
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
This is the most important change in the round.

Every version of the heavy-condition message — on /book, on /call, in the CSR
script, on the landing pages — said the same two things: the price covers a
standard clean, and anything extra is agreed before the crew starts. Both
true. Neither answers the question the customer is actually asking, which is:

    "So what happens if I say no?"

Left unanswered, the customer supplies their own answer, and the answer they
supply is "they leave and I have no cleaner the day before my inspection." So
a disclosure written to build trust was landing as a threat.

They can decline and still get the clean they booked, at the quoted price.
That clause is now in all six places the policy is stated:

  js/rp-messages.js       price.flaggedBody   (new entry)
  book/index.html         questionnaire note
  book/index.html         estimate condition panel
  call/index.html         quoteSayLine() spoken caveat
  call/index.html         "So the price could go up?" objection answer
  9 static pages          the re-quote sentences in the price bands

It costs the business nothing to say, because it is what the arrival SOP is
supposed to do anyway. (That SOP is still unbuilt — see OPEN RISK below.)


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. "INSIDE AND OUT" WAS SELLING SOMETHING WE DO NOT SELL
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The move-out includes screen was headed "Inspection-ready, inside and out".
This is an interior clean. Exterior windows and the garage floor are paid
add-ons — and they are the literal next screen.

So the customer read "inside and out", formed the reasonable belief that the
outside was included, and was asked to pay for the outside two taps later.
That is a manufactured objection: we created it in a heading and then charged
for it. The fine print on that same screen already named those exclusions
correctly, so the screen was contradicting itself top to bottom.

FIX: "Every room, ready for the walkthrough." Same energy, no promise to walk
back.

Note: "appliances inside and out" on the landing pages is correct and was left
alone — that means the inside and outside of the appliance, which is true.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. THE ESTIMATE CARD, REORDERED
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Two things were in the wrong place.

THE CAVEAT WAS BELOW THE PROMISE. The flagged note sat under the guarantee
panel and under the line-item breakdown. So the screen promised "this is the
price" in a red panel and then, two blocks later, explained that the price
might change. In that order the guarantee reads like something the fine print
is quietly walking back — and a customer who stops reading at the red panel,
which is most of them, had been told the reassuring half and not the true one.

Caveat first now, promise second. Both honest, nothing below contradicting
anything above.

THE DEPOSIT MATH WAS BURIED INSIDE THE GUARANTEE. It was the quiet grey second
line inside the red panel, which put the two strongest arguments on the page in
one container and let the weaker-looking one absorb the stronger. They are not
the same argument:

    guarantee = risk reversal. "If the landlord flags it, we come back."
    deposit   = value.         "This costs less than half what you're protecting."

One answers "what if it goes wrong", the other answers "is this worth it at
all" — and the second is the question standing between this customer and the
button. It now sits directly above the CTA, the last thing read before the
tap, which is the job it was written for. The guarantee panel keeps the
guarantee alone and dropped a wrapper div in the process.

FINAL ORDER:
    price
    4 bed · 2 bath                    <- what they have
    2 cleaners · 8 hours on site      <- what the price buys
    inspection-ready line
    condition panel (only if flagged)
    guarantee
    breakdown (only if add-ons)
    ── rule ──
    deposit comparison
    pay-after note
    Book This Cleaning
    Text me this quote instead
    trust line
    fine print (only if NOT flagged)

THE SPEC LINE SPLIT. It ran "4 bed · 2 bath · 2 cleaners · 8 hours" as one
dot-joined string. Two different kinds of fact: the first pair is what the
customer has and already knows, the second is what the price buys and is the
only part that argues for the number. Joined together, the part doing the
selling was the tail of a string the reader had already started skipping.
Two lines now, the second quieter, and "8 hours" became "8 hours on site".


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. TWO DIFFERENT THINGS WERE BOTH CALLED "FLAGGED" AND LOOKED IDENTICAL
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
There are two flagged outcomes in this funnel and they mean opposite things:

    water off / A/C out / mold  ->  BLOCKED. No price possible. Must call.
    heavy trash or grime        ->  PRICED, with a caveat. Book away.

Both were drawn as amber panels with a warning triangle. So the heavy-condition
customer — the common case, who has a price and can book — was shown a warning
triangle on the screen where they were about to tap Book, and read it as
"something is wrong with your booking", which is the opposite of what it says.

COLOUR NOW CARRIES THE MEANING, one each:

    red      guarantee, and the brand
    amber    we cannot price this; we need you to call     (blocked screen only)
    neutral  heads-up, priced, nothing for you to do       (condition panels)

A circled `i` icon was added to the set so the warning triangle means exactly
one thing. The blocked screen's "Needs a look" row is now the only amber in
the funnel, which is correct: it is the reason that screen has a phone number
instead of a Book button.

AND THE HEAVY-CONDITION CUSTOMER GOT A CALL OPTION. Their only exits were Book
and Text-me-the-quote. A genuinely rough home is the case where the customer
most wants a human and the business most wants the call — it is where the real
number gets set and where an arrival surcharge stops being a surprise. There
is now an inline "Or call us and we'll price it now — (580) 215-0915" inside
the panel. Inline, not a second button, so it never competes with Book. No lead
post on it: the contact gate runs before the estimate, so they are already in
the CRM.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6. THEME CONSISTENCY
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE "SECOND OPTION" ROLE HAD TWO SHAPES. The blocked screen drew it as a
bordered full-width button; the estimate drew the same role — the other way to
reach us — as a 28px underlined link with 13px of padding bolted on in round 55
to clear the tap-target minimum. Both use .rp-secondary-cta now. The rule
gained font-family, cursor, appearance and an .is-sent state so it works on the
estimate's <button>, which relabels itself on tap. .rp-text-link is deleted
outright rather than left unused — five rules with no markup left to style is
how a stylesheet ends up with two ways to draw one thing.

HEADING TREATMENT. Both screens are .rp-est cards now, so .rp-blocked-h is
sized explicitly to the same serif scale rather than left to inherit. A 30px
serif heading on one card and a 24px one on the next is drift nobody can name
and everybody notices.

ONE SHAPE FOR "READ THIS, NOTHING IS WRONG". .rp-quest-note (questionnaire),
.rp-est-note.is-condition (estimate) and .rp-included-warn (new, see 7) are
the same neutral panel with the same circled-i icon, on three different
screens.

KEPT DELIBERATELY: the blocked screen reuses .rp-est-line for its recap rows.
That shared row style is why it reads as part of the product rather than an
error page. Not something for a later pass to tidy up.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
7. THE ONE MISTAKE THE FUNNEL MUST NOT LET SOMEBODY MAKE
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Booking three hours of Basic to pass a housing inspection is the worst outcome
available here, and it is a quiet one: the clean will be good, the crew will do
everything asked, and the inspection fails on the oven and the cabinet
interiors nobody bought. No refund conversation fixes that and no review
survives it.

The warning did exist — as the last sentence of a four-sentence fine-print
paragraph under a Continue button, which is functionally nowhere. It is now its
own panel with an icon, above the fine print, on Deep and Basic only:

    "This is hourly cleaning, not a move-out. If you need to pass an
     inspection, book Move-Out Cleaning instead."

(RP_MSG.timed.notMoveoutShort — new entry, so both surfaces read one source.)

8. SMALLER COPY FIXES
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PROMISE ORDER FLIPPED, everywhere. Was "Standard home in standard condition —
the price you see is the price you pay", which opens on the caveat and closes
on the promise, so a skimmer reads the hedge and leaves. Now "The price you see
is the price you pay — for a standard home in standard condition." Same claim,
same words, reassurance first. Applied to RP_MSG.price.promise and to the
promise strip on 9 static pages.

BLOCKED SCREEN OVER-PROMISED. "One call or text and we'll sort it today"
promises a same-day answer to a text sent at 9pm on a Sunday. A small broken
promise on first contact costs more than the urgency it buys. Now "we'll get
you a real number fast". Also "this home" rather than "it" — "it" was pointing
at the reason, not the house.

CONTACT GATE HAD A DANGLING PRONOUN. "And where should we send it?" — nothing
on that screen says what "it" is. Now "Where should we send your quote?"

CSR CHECKBOX WAS TELLING LIZ SOMETHING UNTRUE. The heavy-condition toggle was
labelled "doesn't change this quote" — true of the number on that screen and
false about the job. A CSR who reads that as cosmetic ticks it and moves on
without reading the caveat. Now: "quote stays the same; crew re-checks on
arrival, so read the caveat."

OPEN RISK, CARRIED FORWARD FROM ROUND 52 AND NOW LARGER
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The arrival-walkthrough re-quote SOP is still unbuilt, and this round made six
more places on the website promise how it behaves — including the new promise
that a customer can DECLINE the extra and still receive the standard clean at
the quoted price.

That promise is now on the estimate screen, in Liz's script, in the objection
answers and on nine landing pages. If a crew ever arrives at a heavy home,
quotes extra, hears no, and leaves, the site is on record promising otherwise.
The crew needs three things written down: what counts as beyond standard, who
they call before touching anything, and that a declined re-quote means they do
the standard clean anyway. The margins rest on this and so, now, does the copy.
