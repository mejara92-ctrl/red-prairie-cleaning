THE HEADLINE: THE HEAVY SURCHARGE WAS NEVER CHARGING ANYTHING
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
This was meant to be a rate change from 20% to 25%. It turned out to be
switching the mechanism on for the first time.

RP_CONDITION_PRICED_SERVICES was an empty array, so rpConditionMultiplier()
returned 0 for every service and the 0.20/0.50 entries in
RP_CONDITION_MULTIPLIER were dead constants. Worse, there were two fields
describing condition that never met: /book wrote a BOOLEAN
(moveoutHeavyCondition) from the questionnaire, and rpConditionKey() read a
STRING (rpState.condition) that only /call's old step ever set. So even if
the list had been populated, /book's answer would still have priced at zero.

A customer who told us the home had heavy buildup got the standard price, and
the crew was expected to renegotiate on arrival -- against an SOP the engine's
own comments call "the single biggest open risk against this ladder" and
"still unbuilt".

    size      standard   heavy    delta
    1-2 bed   $199       $249     +$50
    3   bed   $299       $374     +$75
    4   bed   $399       $499     +$100
    5   bed   $499       $624     +$125

Margin percentage is unchanged by construction (labour is 40% of the ticket
and the guarantee reserve 8% of it, so both scale). What the surcharge buys is
the extra crew-hours a heavy home takes, which previously came out of margin.

The surcharge rounds to the whole dollar. 25% of the ladder is $49.75/$74.75/
$99.75/$124.75, and a stray 75 cents on the one line item a customer is
already primed to be suspicious of reads as a formula applied to them.

TWO PIECES OF COPY HAD TO GO, not be adjusted. /book's questionnaire note
opened "Your price still stands for a standard clean", and /call's script said
the quote "covers a standard clean" and the crew might ask for more on
arrival. Both were true while Heavy cost nothing. Left in place they would
have sat directly above a line item contradicting them -- and /call's version
would have described charging the caller twice.

THE TRADE THE NEW COPY MAKES, which the business now has to keep: declare
Heavy and the price is FIRM, no arrival re-quote. Declare Standard on a home
that isn't and the crew re-prices before starting. A percentage surcharge on a
self-reported question gives people a reason to lie; making honesty the
cheaper option is the only durable answer. That means the arrival-walkthrough
SOP is now load-bearing, not optional.


THE MENU: TWO GROUPS, NOT ONE LIST OF FIVE
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
    CLEANING YOUR HOME          MOVING OUT, OR CARPETS ONLY
      Basic Cleaning   $120       Move-Out Cleaning  $199+  [Most Popular]
      Deep Cleaning    $240       Carpet Cleaning    $50/room
      Whole-Home Reset $480

The flat list asked the customer to compare "3 hours" against "your whole
house passes inspection" as though those were the same kind of purchase. They
are not even priced the same way.

The grouping also solves what the re-order costs. Move-Out is the best product
this business sells -- $199-$499 at 27-39% margin against Basic's 21.9%, it
carries the deposit guarantee, and every landing page and PCS search funnels
into it. Dropping it to fourth in a flat list buries it. As the sole headline
of its own group it reads first within that group, so the ladder that was
asked for and the prominence the P&L wants stop competing.

"Most Popular" stayed on Move-Out and was NOT moved to Deep. Deep is featured
(visual weight, no claim); Move-Out is genuinely the most-booked product, and
balancing the two groups visually is not worth a false badge.

HOURLY CLEANING IS OFF THE PUBLIC MENU (RP_HOURLY_PUBLIC = false). There is no
service called "organizing clean" -- the only place this site says "Organizing,
decluttering & light tidying" is rpIncludes.hourly's item list, so that row was
the instruction's target. It should have gone anyway: since round 52 dropped
Hourly to $40/hr it has been Basic with the scope removed at an identical
price, and nobody should pick it. Not deleted -- Liz can still book it on
/call, so a caller who wants to direct their own scope still gets it.


THE WHOLE-HOME RESET: $480, 2 CLEANERS x 6 HOURS
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Priced as a time block at $40/hr, which is exactly what a Deep with the
second-cleaner add-on already cost. That is why it was safe to add: it
introduces no new price, only a name and a menu row for a configuration the
engine could already produce. 46.9% margin, and the minimum-wage floor does not
trip until 26.5 crew-hours against the 12 it is budgeted at.

IT COSTS MORE THAN THE $399 FOUR-BEDROOM MOVE-OUT, which covers the same rooms.
This was flagged before it was built and shipped anyway, with a separator that
makes it work: a MOVE-OUT REQUIRES AN EMPTY HOUSE. Someone still living in
their home cannot buy the move-out product, so the two never compete. Every
customer-facing line -- the menu card, rpIncludes.reset, /pricing, and Liz's
objection script -- leads with the occupied home, not with scope. Scope is an
argument against the move-out that cannot be won.

Named "reset" rather than "extreme" because rpConditionOrder already uses
"Extreme Buildup" as a CONDITION label, and a customer with a normally-dirty
house reads "extreme" as "not me".


ONE TABLE FOR EVERY SERVICE THAT SELLS TIME
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Basic, Deep and Reset are the same product in three sizes: N hours x C cleaners
at $40/hr. That shape used to be two parallel pairs of constants and an
`if (deep) ... if (maintenance) ...` branch in SIX functions. Adding a third
rung that way means a third branch in all six, and one missed is a wrong price
on a live booking -- which is not hypothetical, the round-52 note on
rpSecondCleanerAnchorHours records exactly that bug.

RP_TIMED_SERVICES is now the one description. The published RP_*_ANCHOR_*
constants stay literal (tools/check-prices.py greps for them), and
rpTimedServicesAgree() asserts the two match.

A BUG THIS INTRODUCED AND THE TEST SUITE THEN CAUGHT: Extra Time was
`hours * $40` flat, correct only while every timed service had one cleaner.
On the two-cleaner Reset that sold an hour of a two-person crew for one
person's price -- $560 for 16 crew-hours, an effective $35/crew-hour. It now
bills the service's base crew. The test asserts the RATIO ($40 per crew-hour in
every configuration) rather than three expected totals, because $560 is a
perfectly plausible number and no total-based check would have called it wrong.


THE BASIC / DEEP / RESET SCREEN, REBUILT
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THREE THINGS WERE WRONG AND THEY COMPOUNDED:

(1) IT HEDGED FOUR TIMES AND DESCRIBED ONCE. The intro hedged ("the same way
    our hourly service works"), the outcome hedged ("time, not a finished
    checklist"), a hardcoded panel hedged ("this is hourly cleaning, not a
    move-out"), and the fine print hedged again while re-explaining two other
    services. Four consecutive disclaimers on the screen whose job is to say
    what you are buying. One caveat now, at full strength -- the round-52
    reasoning for that framing still holds, it just stopped being said four
    times.

(2) "ADD HOURS OR A SECOND CLEANER" WAS NOT A BUTTON. It was a static chip in
    the same row as two facts, and the real control was two screens later
    behind the contact gate. The worst interaction in the funnel: it
    advertised the exact affordance the customer wanted, where they wanted it,
    and did nothing. It is a live stepper now, on this screen.

(3) THE SCOPE WAS INVISIBLE. rpIncludes.items has been carried since round 16
    and rendered on /book for nobody -- only /call's CSR rail read it. So "does
    this include the bathroom" was answerable on the phone and not on the page.

    NOW:   5 hours of cleaning
           Kitchen, bathrooms, floors and the everyday surfaces.
           [ $200 ]  5 hours · 1 cleaner · $40/hour
           WHERE THE TIME USUALLY GOES: (the five items, two columns)
           Hours of cleaning   3 included + 2 added    [-] 5 [+]
           [ ] Add a second cleaner      Same hours, twice the hands · +$200

THE FREQUENCY SCREEN IS GONE, THE SELECTION IS NOT
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Four full-width rows showing an identical $120 under a subheading that said, in
so many words, that the choice did not matter. Round 52 removed the discounts
and left the screen standing.

But visitsPerMonth and the required first Deep both read it, so defaulting
everyone to One-Time would have silently dropped the recurring signal -- the
highest-LTV thing this funnel captures -- to save a tap. It is a checkbox plus
three pills on the timed screen now. Nine screens became eight.

Checking it opens on Biweekly rather than leaving frequency null, so the
booking is never in a state that says "recurring" but cannot say how often.

ALSO FIXED HERE: a recurring Basic's first visit is a required Deep, so the
customer pays $240 on booking day and $120 after. The old screen mentioned that
in fine print under a button. The price card states it.

ON PREPAY, WHICH WAS RAISED AND IS THE RIGHT INSTINCT: a recurring discount
buys nothing (they can cancel next week); prepay is real commitment. But the
math does not work on Basic -- 10% off a prepaid 4-pack is $432, dropping
per-visit profit to $14.24, a 12% margin against a 20% target. If it ships, it
ships on Deep, or on Basic at no discount sold on convenience. Not built.


THE UGLY BUTTONS: ONE CSS PROPERTY
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
.rp-quest-list was `align-items: center` on a column flexbox, so each row
shrank to fit its widest child -- the QUESTION LABEL, not the buttons. The pill
grid then inherited whatever width the sentence above it happened to need.
"Is the water on?" got a narrow pair; "Is the home free of mold or pest
issues?" got a wide one. `max-width: 260px` only capped that; it never set it.

Now --rp-quest-width (320px) sizes the list, the rows stretch to it, and both
pill variants fill it. Measured in a browser after the fix: 312, 312, 312, 312.

The condition question also gets a rule above it and a red "THIS ONE AFFECTS
YOUR PRICE" eyebrow, because it is now the only priced answer on a screen of
logistics questions. An extra screen was considered and rejected -- the
instruction for this round was that the site be simpler, and this funnel has
regretted buying clarity with a tap three times (rounds 31, 52, and the
frequency screen above).


EXTERIOR WINDOWS: THE NUMBERS WERE FINE, THE MODEL WASN'T
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
It was one flat price per HOUSE with no size input anywhere. An 8-window duplex
and a 30-window two-storey both paid $100. On the small one that is excellent
money; on the large one it is a loss, and this business publishes prices for
homes up to 3,400+ sq ft.

Basic/Premium was also the wrong axis: "Premium = + screens" valued screen
removal at $100, and nobody can tell from the words which one is theirs.

    NOW:  $6 per window, $100 minimum
          screens +$3 per window
          two storey +40%

          10 windows, glass only            $100  (floor)
          24 windows, glass only            $144
          24 windows + screens              $216
          32 + screens, two storey          $403

The floor protects the drive-out -- eight windows at $6 is $48 and nobody
should drive out for that. It applies BEFORE the two-storey uplift, or a small
two-storey job would get its ladder work free.

THE TWO-STOREY QUESTION MATTERS MORE THAN THE SURCHARGE. Second-floor exteriors
mean ladder work, which is a different safety and insurance conversation. The
question exists so the crew knows before the van is loaded; it is flagged in
capitals on /call's add-on row. If ladder work should ever stop being offered,
that field is where it gets turned off.

Windows also came OFF Basic's add-on list. A $100-and-up exterior job attached
to a $120 interior cleaning was the most lopsided pairing on the screen -- the
add-on could cost more than the service.

The webhook keeps the field name exterior_windows_tier (no GHL migration) but
its value is now the human summary, e.g. "19-28 windows · screens in · two
storey", with three raw fields added beside it for filtering.


GARAGE FLOOR WASH: $150 -> $100
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
As instructed. $60 left after the 40% labour share covers degreaser on a normal
floor, and there is no separate drive-out. What it does not cover is a garage

THREE THINGS NOBODY ASKED ABOUT
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
* js/rp-canonical-estimator.js DELETED (and tools/apply-canonical-estimator.py
  with it). 524 lines containing Deep $249, Basic $149, carpet $75/room and
  "Factory Reset(TM) Move-Out Cleaning". Nothing loaded it -- it had been
  dereferenced for several rounds and noted as "harmless" -- but a file named
  "canonical" full of wrong prices is a landmine for whoever reads it next.

* CARPET-ONLY BOOKINGS WERE PROMISED A LANDLORD GUARANTEE. rpGuaranteeType()
  fell past every branch to `return "deposit"`, so a carpet job with no
  landlord and no inspection anywhere near it showed "if your landlord flags
  something we missed, we come back free". The round log had carried this as a
  known oddity for fourteen rounds. It is "satisfaction" now, and the default
  for an unknown service key is the promise we can always keep.

* /pricing's structured data said lowPrice "150", stale from the old $149
  Basic. The real floor is $50. That is the number Google reads.


CAUGHT BY LOOKING AT THE PAGE, NOT BY A TEST
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Three defects survived a green test suite and were found by driving /book in a
headless browser and reading screenshots. Worth recording, because the pattern
is the same each time: the tests check numbers, and these were not numbers.

* THE CONTACT GATE UNDERSOLD A HEAVY HOME. It read rpServiceBasePrice(), which
  is the price BEFORE the surcharge, so a heavy four-bedroom was shown "$399",
  asked for a name and phone number, and then showed $499. The single worst
  place in the funnel for that gap. Now reads the real total and names the
  surcharge in the same sentence. A test was added -- it greps /book's source,
  which is ugly and better than trusting the next person to remember.

* THE INTRO CONTRADICTED THE HEADING. The first version of the rebuilt screen
  put hours and crew in rpIncludes.intro too, so tapping [+] twice produced
  "5 hours of cleaning" as a heading with "Three hours with one cleaner"
  underneath it. `intro` describes SCOPE only now. Same rule that deleted
  `highlights`, arrived at twice: a hardcoded string restating a computed
  value will eventually contradict it.

* "$undefined" ON THE WINDOWS CARD. The add-on card's teaser still read
  rpAddonCatalog.windows.basic, a key that no longer exists.

Also fixed: a duplicate arrow on the new CTA (.rp-primary already appends one
via ::after), and "$40/hour each" printed against "1 cleaner".
