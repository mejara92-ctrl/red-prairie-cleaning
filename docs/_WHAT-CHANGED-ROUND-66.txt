THE HEADLINE: THE HEAVY SURCHARGE WAS NEVER CHARGING ANYTHING
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
This was meant to be a rate change from 20% to 25%. It turned out to be
switching the mechanism on for the first time.

RP_CONDITION_PRICED_SERVICES was an empty array, so rpConditionMultiplier()
returned 0 for every service and the 0.20/0.50 entries in
RP_CONDITION_MULTIPLIER were dead constants. Worse, there were two fields
describing condition that never met: /book wrote a BOOLEAN
(moveoutHeavyCondition) from the questionnaire, and rpConditionKey() read a
STRING (rpState.condition) that only /call's old step ever set. So even if
the list had been populated, /book's answer would still have priced at zero.

A customer who told us the home had heavy buildup got the standard price, and
the crew was expected to renegotiate on arrival -- against an SOP the engine's
own comments call "the single biggest open risk against this ladder" and
"still unbuilt".

    size      standard   heavy    delta
    1-2 bed   $199       $249     +$50
    3   bed   $299       $374     +$75
    4   bed   $399       $499     +$100
    5   bed   $499       $624     +$125

Margin percentage is unchanged by construction (labour is 40% of the ticket
and the guarantee reserve 8% of it, so both scale). What the surcharge buys is
the extra crew-hours a heavy home takes, which previously came out of margin.

The surcharge rounds to the whole dollar. 25% of the ladder is $49.75/$74.75/
$99.75/$124.75, and a stray 75 cents on the one line item a customer is
already primed to be suspicious of reads as a formula applied to them.

TWO PIECES OF COPY HAD TO GO, not be adjusted. /book's questionnaire note
opened "Your price still stands for a standard clean", and /call's script said
the quote "covers a standard clean" and the crew might ask for more on
arrival. Both were true while Heavy cost nothing. Left in place they would
have sat directly above a line item contradicting them -- and /call's version
would have described charging the caller twice.

THE TRADE THE NEW COPY MAKES, which the business now has to keep: declare
Heavy and the price is FIRM, no arrival re-quote. Declare Standard on a home
that isn't and the crew re-prices before starting. A percentage surcharge on a
self-reported question gives people a reason to lie; making honesty the
cheaper option is the only durable answer. That means the arrival-walkthrough
SOP is now load-bearing, not optional.


THE MENU: TWO GROUPS, NOT ONE LIST OF FIVE
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
    CLEANING YOUR HOME          MOVING OUT, OR CARPETS ONLY
      Basic Cleaning   $120       Move-Out Cleaning  $199+  [Most Popular]
      Deep Cleaning    $240       Carpet Cleaning    $50/room
      Whole-Home Reset $480

The flat list asked the customer to compare "3 hours" against "your whole
house passes inspection" as though those were the same kind of purchase. They
are not even priced the same way.

The grouping also solves what the re-order costs. Move-Out is the best product
this business sells -- $199-$499 at 27-39% margin against Basic's 21.9%, it
carries the deposit guarantee, and every landing page and PCS search funnels
into it. Dropping it to fourth in a flat list buries it. As the sole headline
of its own group it reads first within that group, so the ladder that was
asked for and the prominence the P&L wants stop competing.

"Most Popular" stayed on Move-Out and was NOT moved to Deep. Deep is featured
(visual weight, no claim); Move-Out is genuinely the most-booked product, and
balancing the two groups visually is not worth a false badge.

HOURLY CLEANING IS OFF THE PUBLIC MENU (RP_HOURLY_PUBLIC = false). There is no
service called "organizing clean" -- the only place this site says "Organizing,
decluttering & light tidying" is rpIncludes.hourly's item list, so that row was
the instruction's target. It should have gone anyway: since round 52 dropped
Hourly to $40/hr it has been Basic with the scope removed at an identical
price, and nobody should pick it. Not deleted -- Liz can still book it on
/call, so a caller who wants to direct their own scope still gets it.


THE WHOLE-HOME RESET: $480, 2 CLEANERS x 6 HOURS
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Priced as a time block at $40/hr, which is exactly what a Deep with the
second-cleaner add-on already cost. That is why it was safe to add: it
introduces no new price, only a name and a menu row for a configuration the
engine could already produce. 46.9% margin, and the minimum-wage floor does not
trip until 26.5 crew-hours against the 12 it is budgeted at.

IT COSTS MORE THAN THE $399 FOUR-BEDROOM MOVE-OUT, which covers the same rooms.
This was flagged before it was built and shipped anyway, with a separator that
makes it work: a MOVE-OUT REQUIRES AN EMPTY HOUSE. Someone still living in
their home cannot buy the move-out product, so the two never compete. Every
customer-facing line -- the menu card, rpIncludes.reset, /pricing, and Liz's
objection script -- leads with the occupied home, not with scope. Scope is an
argument against the move-out that cannot be won.

Named "reset" rather than "extreme" because rpConditionOrder already uses
"Extreme Buildup" as a CONDITION label, and a customer with a normally-dirty
house reads "extreme" as "not me".


ONE TABLE FOR EVERY SERVICE THAT SELLS TIME
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Basic, Deep and Reset are the same product in three sizes: N hours x C cleaners
at $40/hr. That shape used to be two parallel pairs of constants and an
`if (deep) ... if (maintenance) ...` branch in SIX functions. Adding a third
rung that way means a third branch in all six, and one missed is a wrong price
on a live booking -- which is not hypothetical, the round-52 note on
rpSecondCleanerAnchorHours records exactly that bug.

RP_TIMED_SERVICES is now the one description. The published RP_*_ANCHOR_*
constants stay literal (tools/check-prices.py greps for them), and
rpTimedServicesAgree() asserts the two match.

A BUG THIS INTRODUCED AND THE TEST SUITE THEN CAUGHT: Extra Time was
`hours * $40` flat, correct only while every timed service had one cleaner.
On the two-cleaner Reset that sold an hour of a two-person crew for one
person's price -- $560 for 16 crew-hours, an effective $35/crew-hour. It now
bills the service's base crew. The test asserts the RATIO ($40 per crew-hour in
every configuration) rather than three expected totals, because $560 is a
perfectly plausible number and no total-based check would have called it wrong.

CAR DETAILING AS A BOOKABLE SERVICE
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
$120 / 3 hrs standard, $240 / 6 hrs deep, pet hair +$50 — the same $40 an hour
everything else on this price list sells, which is the reason it works: the
tiers are just hours, and the engine already had rpCarDetailPrice().

THE SERVICE KEY IS "cardetailing"; THE ADD-ON KEY IS "cardetail". Deliberately
different, and asserted in the test suite. rpAddonAvailable() is keyed on the
add-on name, so a collision would let the standalone service offer itself as
its own add-on and bill twice — and $240 for a $120 detail is a plausible
enough number that no total-based check would have flagged it.

Both paths share rpState.carDetail / carDetailPetHair and one price function,
so a $290 detail costs $290 whichever way it was bought. The test asserts
alone-vs-attached parity across all four configurations.

Its flow has no add-ons step (nothing bolts onto a car), which means
rpCurrentFlow() inserts the contact gate before "estimate" — the fallback that
branch was written for, exercised for the first time.

MARGIN: 21.9% on the standard tier, the thinnest ticket on the list and tied
with Basic. It clears the 20% target and the wage floor, but note what the
model does NOT capture: a standalone detail is a whole trip for the smallest
job we sell, with no house clean to share the drive-out with. That is why both
the booking screen and Liz's script push the cross-sell — same price, one
trip — rather than selling the standalone version harder.

ROUND 67 — MARKETING TAGS, AND A CAR-DETAILING LANDING PAGE

"Shouldn't we add tags to see what kind of move-out they ordered so that we
 can sell them promotions, reminders? idk if we do that."

THE ANSWER TO "IDK IF WE DO THAT": WE DID NOT, AND THE GAP WAS BACKWARDS
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The site already sent HighLevel forty-odd CUSTOM FIELDS on every booking --
bedroom count, heavy condition, every add-on, rental vs selling. None of it
was a TAG.

That distinction is the whole problem. A custom field is something you read
once you have already found a contact. A tag is what you filter a smart list
by and what a workflow triggers on. The only tags that existed were in
workers/PATCH-lead-endpoint.md, and they tag people who did NOT book
("website-partial-lead", "call-no-booking").

So the business could automate follow-up to everyone who walked away, and
nothing at all to anyone who paid.

WHAT SHIPPED, AFTER A CORRECTION: the first version had five tag families --
svc-*, moveout-*, bought-*, plan-*, plus has-pets and two-story -- about
twenty tags in the vocabulary. Direct instruction cutting it back:

  "I don't need a million tags, just basic-clean, deep-clean, move-out,
   basic-detail, deep-detail. and i'll remarket to people getting details and
   cleans, ignoring the move-out cause their gone."

Five tags now, and the test asserts them as a CLOSED SET rather than spot-
checking a few -- a sixth tag fails a test, because the value of five tags is
that they stay five.

    basic-clean     Basic Cleaning
    deep-clean      Deep Cleaning, and Whole-Home Reset
    move-out        Move-Out Cleaning
    basic-detail    standard 3-hour car detail
    deep-detail     deep 6-hour car detail

THE CUT WAS RIGHT, and the reasoning is worth keeping: a tag earns its
permanent place on a contact record only if a campaign would actually segment
by it. Twenty tags nobody filters on is twenty columns of noise on every
customer forever. Nothing was lost -- bedroom count, heavy vs standard, which
add-ons, recurring cadence and the rest all still ship as CUSTOM FIELDS on the
same payload, and a HighLevel smart list can filter those directly. They
stopped pretending to be segments.

A BOOKING CAN CARRY TWO. The detail tags are the car-detail options, not a
service-vs-add-on split, so a Deep Cleaning with a deep car detail is both
"deep-clean" and "deep-detail" -- two purchases with two repeat clocks. That
is the case a service-only scheme would miss, and most detailing will sell as
an add-on rather than standalone.

TWO PLACEMENTS NEEDED A DECISION, since five tags cover seven products:
  Whole-Home Reset -> deep-clean. It is the deepest clean we sell and a Reset
    customer is a deep-clean remarketing target. A sixth tag would have
    re-grown what this cut removed.
  Carpet-only -> NO TAG. Neither a clean nor a detail. Flagged in
    TAGS-AND-CAMPAIGNS.md rather than silently decided -- carpet is the most
    naturally annual thing on the price list and is now invisible to every
    campaign. One line to add if wanted.

MOVE-OUT IS TAGGED BUT NOT MARKETED TO, by instruction -- they have left the
address. The tag still ships because it is what a SUPPRESSION list filters on:
without it there is no way to exclude move-out contacts from a campaign aimed
at everyone else.

ONE SUPPRESSION LEFT FOR THE GHL SIDE: recurring customers get basic-clean
like everyone else, and "book your next clean" lands badly on someone whose
next clean is already scheduled. No tag for it -- filter the `frequency`
custom field to exclude anything but One-Time.

workers/TAGS-AND-CAMPAIGNS.md documents the vocabulary, seven campaigns, and
the Worker change needed to apply them. ONE THING TO GET RIGHT THERE:
HighLevel's contact upsert REPLACES the tag list. A repeat customer who booked
a move-out last year and a Basic today should end up with both tags -- that
history is exactly what makes the segments valuable -- so the Worker has to
union with the existing tags or use the add-tags endpoint.

TWO BUGS FOUND WHILE WIRING THE PAYLOAD
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
* addon_windows_tier was still reading rpState.windowsTier, the legacy
  basic/premium field that nothing has written since windows went per-window
  in round 66. Every booking since then reported "None" for a window job that
  was actually sold. Now reads the live summary, with a price field beside it.

* Car detailing had NO webhook fields at all. It shipped in round 66c and the
  office could not see which option was sold, or whether pet hair was on it.

A GAP LEFT OPEN ON PURPOSE: moveout-rental / moveout-selling only ever
populate from /call, because /book never asks why they are moving. It is one
tap and it splits the move-out list into two genuinely different audiences --
a renter chasing a deposit and a seller staging a house want different things.
Not added unprompted: it is a new question in a funnel this round has been
busy shortening, and that is a conversion judgement, not a code one.

THE LANDING PAGE: /interior-car-detailing-lawton-ok/
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Built by cloning the carpet page's shell -- head, nav, footer, sticky CTA,
tracking -- so the design system, CSS and Ads conversion wiring are identical
rather than reimplemented. Only the body content and the page-specific
metadata are new.

Structured data: a Service node with all three real prices as Offers, a
BreadcrumbList, and a seven-question FAQPage. All of it valid JSON-LD.

The copy leads with the pricing model rather than adjectives, because the
model IS the pitch: $40 an hour for a detailer, the same rate as a cleaner in
the house, which is why there are two options instead of ten packages. It also
says plainly what we do NOT do -- no exterior wash, wax, paint correction,
engine bays or headlights -- since that is the first question a detailing
search has and pretending otherwise wins a booking we would do badly.

It pushes the cross-sell rather than the standalone sale: same price either
way, but attached to a cleaning it is one trip instead of two. A standalone
detail is a whole visit for the thinnest ticket on the price list (21.9%,
tied with Basic), so the page is honest about which is the better buy.

The reviews section says out loud that the reviews shown are for cleaning
work, not detailing. Inventing detailing reviews for a service that launched
this month is the one thing that would undermine a 5.0 rating built on real
ones.

Wired in: linked from the footer of all 18 pages that list services (the
round-66c footer link pointed at /pricing#addons and now points here),
self-link suppressed on the page itself, added to sitemap.xml, and every
inherited /book?service=carpet CTA retargeted to ?service=cardetailing --
three of them, which is the kind of thing cloning a shell always leaves
behind and a rendered-page check always catches.

Copy note: "tier" was reworded to "option" throughout. It is jargon a customer
never uses, AND check-prices.py reserves the word for the retired move-out
tiers -- the guard flagged it, and both reasons pointed the same way.
