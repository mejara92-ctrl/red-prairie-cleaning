-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHY IT WAS BAD: IT WAS RENDERING THE DESKTOP BOARD
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
renderPhone() was drawing all seven step cards as an accordion -- one open,
six folded -- plus the desktop side rail stacked underneath. So below the
words Liz needs sat roughly 1,200px of things she does not, including, on the
quote step, a folded card repeating the quote script she was already reading.

It was not a styling problem. A phone shows one thing; the board shows the
whole call. They are different tools.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT "SEPARATE VERSION" MEANS HERE
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Two shells, one brain. The phone shell renders completely different DOM from
the desktop board -- different navigation, different chrome, different dock.
From Liz's side it is a different app.

It is NOT a second file, and that was deliberate. sectionBody(), sectionValid(),
guardrails(), lineFor(), primaryAction(), TITLES, NUDGE and every word of
script are the same functions the board calls. A call/mobile/index.html would
have been two CSR scripts, and inside a month Liz on her phone would have been
quoting a different product from Liz at her desk. That is the exact drift this
codebase has spent 59 rounds preventing in the pricing engine; it applies
harder to the words.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE PHONE SHELL
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
HEADER, one row: caller name, phone, running total, End. The three tools that
used to be crammed in beside them (Explain / Stuck / Look up) moved to the
dock -- at 390px the header held six things and the caller's name wrapped to
three lines.

PROGRESS BAR, sticky, replaces the chip strip:
    STEP 5 OF 7   Give the quote                          All steps
    ────────────────────────────────
The chip strip scrolled horizontally, which meant the chip marked "now" -- the
only one answering "where am I" -- could be the one off the left edge. This
cannot scroll anywhere. Tapping it opens the call sheet.

BODY: one step. The line she says, what to do, that step's controls, its
guardrails. No accordion, no folded cards, no side rail.

DOCK, two rows, fixed:
    [ Steps ] [ Look up ] [ Stuck? ] [ Explain ]
    [ Back ] [        Next: the address        ]
Its height is MEASURED at render and written to --dock-h, because it grows a
reason line (below) and a hard-coded 112px guess had already trapped two
controls behind it once during this round.

CALL SHEET, one tap from either the bar or the dock: the seven steps with
what each one holds and a "You're here" marker, plus the running recap the
desktop keeps in its side column. Tap any step to go there.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE DUMMY-PROOFING
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
IT WILL NOT LET HER WALK PAST A BLANK ANSWER. forwardTarget() finds the next
unfinished step AFTER the current one, which is right on the board: every step
is visible, so a gap she leaves is a gap she can see, and skipping around is
how a real call goes. On a phone the step she left blank scrolls out of
existence the moment she moves, and the next thing that tells her is the
quote -- caller on the line, half-built job, no idea which answer is missing.

So on a phone the big button finishes the current step before offering the
next one. She can still jump anywhere from the call sheet; this governs the
button, not her freedom.

THE DISABLED BUTTON SAYS WHY, on the dock, in amber:
    Bedrooms, bathrooms, and the three checks — Water, A/C, mold or pests.
    Unsure? Answer no.
    [ Back ] [      Finish this step first      ]
A disabled button with no reason beside it is the oldest dead end in software,
and on a phone the control she skipped may be off the top of the screen by the
time she reaches for it.

READABLE AT ARM'S LENGTH. Field labels -- which ARE the questions she reads
out loud -- were 11px uppercase. "Is the air conditioning working?" at caption
size, on a control whose wrong answer quotes an unbookable home. Now 14px
sentence case. Every tap target is 44px or more; inputs are 16px so iOS stops
zooming the page on focus.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
ONE BUG FROM ROUND 58, CAUGHT BY BUILDING THIS
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Round 58 relabelled the condition question on /call to "What shape is the
place in?" to match /book. On /book that is a two-choice pick, so the question
fits its answers. On /call it is still a Yes/No pair -- so the phone screen
read:

    What shape is the place in?     [ Yes ]  [ No ]

Gibberish at a glance, and the kind of thing that gets tapped wrong mid-call.
Now "Has the place seen better days?", which keeps round 58's non-judgmental
framing and is an actual yes/no question. Same field, same boolean.
