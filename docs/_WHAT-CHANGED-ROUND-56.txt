1. "REQUEST A CALLBACK" IS GONE
-------------------------------
Instruction: "I don't see how request a callback will work, just put a
button to call or text red prairie, and have it feed the information
from the home."

Right on both counts. That screen is reached when a home can't be priced
online — water off, A/C out, mold or pests. The person standing in that
house has a problem RIGHT NOW and a move-out date they can't move.
"Request a callback" asked them to do nothing and wait, on a promise
somebody at the office has to remember to keep. Every minute between the
tap and the call back is a minute they spend calling a competitor.

Two buttons now, both reaching a human immediately:

    Call (580) 215-0915
    Text us instead — your details are already in it

THE TEXT BUTTON CARRIES THE HOME WITH IT, which is the part that makes
this better than a form rather than just faster. The message is prefilled:

    Hi Red Prairie - I need a move-out clean and the website couldn't
    price it.
    Home: 4 bed, 2 bath
    Flagged: Water is off, A/C isn't working
    Name: Mike Smith
    Area: Lawton

The first message already says more than most phone calls manage, so the
office can answer with a real number instead of starting the questions
over. The lead is still posted to the CRM the moment either button is
tapped — and unlike the old form, that now fires for callers too, not
just the ones who completed a submit.

The screen also shows the home back to them before the buttons (size,
and what got flagged), so they can see we understood the problem.


2. THE ESTIMATE IS ONE CARD
---------------------------
Instruction: "this screen is goofy, and different from the rest ... make
it simple and like one thing instead of a weird top and a weird bottom."

It was literally two things. The estimate lived in a white card; the
payment note, the CTA, the text link and the fine print lived in the
sticky beige dock that every other screen uses for a single Continue
button. Two containers, two background colours, with the hero photo
showing through the gap between them.

The dock earns its keep when content scrolls past the fold and the action
has to stay reachable. It does not when it holds four elements and half
the screen's copy — at that point it stops being a dock and becomes a
second, differently-coloured screen stuck to the bottom of the first.

Now: price, what it buys, guarantee, breakdown, then a rule, then the
reassurance and the button. One card, read top to bottom, ending on the
thing you do next. The screen returns no footer and the dock does not
render at all.

The blocked screen got the same treatment for the same reason.

Nothing was dropped in the move — the low-availability line, the payment
note, the trust line and the fine print are all still there, just inside
the card with everything else.
