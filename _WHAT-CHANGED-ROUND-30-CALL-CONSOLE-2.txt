RED PRAIRIE — ROUND 30: CALL CONSOLE 2.0
A rewrite of /call's presentation layer. Every line of pricing, booking,
webhook and guardrail logic is carried across unchanged.
========================================================================
Instruction: "Analyze call, is there too much junk in there? Reimagine
call so that it better suits Liz to quickly process information and
assist with sales."

Settings chosen for this build: works on laptop AND phone (touch layout
with keyboard shortcuts layered on), experienced operator (coaching
stripped back hard), and it REPLACES /call outright. The previous
console is preserved verbatim in this zip as call/_legacy-console-1.0.html
if you ever need to look at it — it is not linked from anywhere and not
served as a page.

  Caller                115      y=621         1.00
  Bedrooms               59      y=432         1.00
  Bathrooms              59      y=432         1.00
  Questionnaire          49      y=451         1.00
  Tiers                 364      y=842         1.07
  Add-ons               291      y=1061        1.31
  Quote                 417      y=1268        1.59
  Address                99      y=738         1.00

The quote screen — the exact moment she says a number and then has to go
quiet — carried 541 words of page text and put the Next button 1,268px
down. She scrolled while she talked.

THE JUNK, specifically:

a) THE SAME INSTRUCTION, FOUR TIMES, ON ONE SCREEN.
   On add-ons, "ask them by name" appeared as:
     - the screen subtitle ("Ask carpet, junk, windows and garage by name")
     - the LISTEN row ("Ask these by name. Customers almost never volunteer")
     - inside a 100-word WATCH paragraph
     - a blue guardrail box ("ASK ALL THREE BY NAME")
   (One of those four was mine, added last round. Removing it.)

b) SAY / LISTEN / WATCH WAS A THREE-WAY SPLIT THAT ISN'T A REAL
   DISTINCTION. LISTEN and WATCH both mean "coaching." Under pressure
   there was no way to know which box held the thing you needed, so
   both got skipped.

c) THE RAIL WAS STATIC REFERENCE. The same eight "what's included"
   bullets and the same NOT-INCLUDED paragraph rendered on all twelve
   steps. Needed maybe one call in ten; costing screen space on the
   other nine.

d) TWELVE STEPS, SEVERAL OF THEM ONE TAP. Bedrooms was its own screen.
   Bathrooms was its own screen. Renting-or-selling was its own screen.
   Each transition is a beat of dead air on a live call.

e) NOTHING WAS GLANCEABLE. Everything was prose paragraphs at 14px.
   The running total — the single most valuable number on the page —
   was small text in a side rail.

f) NO WAY TO LOOK ANYTHING UP. "Do I need to be there?" meant opening a
   drawer and eyeballing sixteen entries. "Does that include the oven?"
   meant reading the eight-bullet rail. No search, no index.

g) NO SENSE OF TIME. No call timer at all, on a tool whose own script
   says "pause two or three seconds."


========================================================================
2. WHAT 2.0 IS
========================================================================
One rule: a phone console has two jobs — show the words to SAY, and
capture the answer in one tap. Everything else is reference, and
reference is one keystroke away, never permanently on screen.

FOUR FIXED ZONES, none of which scroll away:
  TOP     caller · call timer · running total (large) · Look up · End
  STRIP   step chips, tap any one to jump
  MAIN    the line to say, then the thing to tap
  DOCK    Back / Next, pinned to the bottom, always reachable

THE LINE. One sentence, in quotation marks, 20-25px serif, alone in its
own card. It is the only thing on screen by default.

COACH, COLLAPSED. LISTEN and WATCH merged into one short paragraph
behind a "Coach" toggle (or the ? key). The setting is remembered, so
someone newer can leave it open permanently and Liz can leave it shut.

GUARDRAILS STAYED — they are the opposite of junk. They only fire when
they apply: this job can't be quoted, the address wasn't read back, the
add-ons just made Inspection Ready the better deal. Two 1.0 guardrails
were deleted because they were unconditional advice about the current
step and are now the first sentence of that step's Coach line ("Say it,
then stop" and "Ask all three by name").

RESULT, same measurements:

                     1.0 words   2.0 words    change
  Service list          271         80        -70%
  Caller                115         66        -43%
  Beds/baths/checks     167         76        -55%   (3 screens -> 1)
  Tiers                 364        103        -72%
  Add-ons               291        159        -45%
  Quote                 417        144        -65%
  Address                99         39        -61%

Every screen is now one viewport or close to it (worst case 1.28), with
the Next button pinned to the bottom of the window rather than 1,268px
down the page.


========================================================================
3. LOOK UP — the feature that let the rail and the drawer go away
========================================================================
Press "/" (or tap Look up) and type a word. One search across:
  - all 16 objections, with the answer AND the coaching note
  - every scope item for the current service ("is the oven included?")
  - every price in the catalog, read live from the pricing engine
  - the policies that were never written down anywhere: deposit and
    payment, do they need to be home, does it need to be empty,
    licensed/insured, what if something's missed, service area,
    rescheduling, and what to do when the water is off

Results are RANKED, not just filtered — a title match beats an answer
match beats a coaching-note match, and shorter entries win ties. Typing
"oven" returns the oven's price and scope first, not a long objection
whose footnote happens to contain the word. Verified:

    "oven"     -> Included: oven, fridge & appliances / Price: oven interior
    "deposit"  -> Policy: deposit or payment up front?
    "pay"      -> Say this: "How do I pay? Do you need a deposit?"
    "not home" -> Policy: do they need to be home?
    "insured"  -> Policy: licensed and insured?
    "spouse"   -> Say this: "I need to think about it / talk to my spouse"

The index is rebuilt on every keystroke from the engine's own constants,
so no price in it can go stale the way a written cheat sheet does.


========================================================================
4. FEWER STEPS
========================================================================
Move-out went from 12 steps to 9.

  "home"    merges bedrooms + bathrooms + the three hard-stop checks.
            They're all just facts about the property, they all fit on
            one screen, and it's the block she reads out as one breath.
  "tiers"   absorbed renting-vs-selling, because that is the only place
            the answer changes what she does: it decides which tier she
            leads with.
  "hourly"  merges cleaners + hours, same reasoning.

ORDER IS UNCHANGED IN EVERY WAY THAT MATTERS. Name and number are still
captured before any price is spoken. The hard-stop checks still land
before the tier screen shows a number. A blocked job still routes to a
callback with the lead already captured.


========================================================================
5. KEYBOARD
========================================================================
For the half of her shifts she's at a laptop. All of it is additive —
every one of these is also a tap target.

  1-9         pick that numbered option on this screen
  Enter       Next (also works from a text field)
  Backspace   Back
  /           open Look up, cursor already in the box
  ?           toggle Coach
  Esc         close whatever is open

Enter inside a TEXTAREA still makes a new line rather than advancing —
verified, because getting that wrong would eat her call notes.


========================================================================
6. ALSO NEW
========================================================================
  - CALL TIMER in the top bar, starting when she picks a service.
  - RUNNING TOTAL is now a large number in the top bar instead of small
    text in a side rail. On a custom-quote job it reads "Custom / Don't
    quote" in amber instead of a dollar figure.
  - THE SIDE PANEL is now a live recap of what she has captured — every
    add-on as its own line with its price — which is the thing she reads
    back at the end of the call. It replaced the static scope bullets.
  - DARK MODE, because a console someone stares at for a whole shift
    should follow the machine.
  - The console works down to 320px with no horizontal scrolling, and
    every control clears the 44px touch minimum except the step chips
    (34px, secondary navigation, well clear of the 24px WCAG floor).

7. WHAT DID NOT CHANGE — and how that was proven
========================================================================
Every piece of business logic was carried across verbatim: the shared
pricing engine integration, guardrails(), the GHL booking payload,
the custom-quote lead path, outcome logging, the calendar and its
slot-fetching, localStorage persistence mid-call, the CSR identity, the
password gate, jobDetails() for the crew sheet, and all 16 objections
with their answers and notes.

PROOF, not assertion. The same call was driven through 1.0 and through
2.0 with byte-identical state, and the POST body sent to the booking
worker was captured from both and diffed:

========================================================================
8. THINGS I DELIBERATELY DID NOT DO
========================================================================
- The password is still a plaintext constant in the page source. That is
  unchanged from 1.0 and it's fine for what it is (keeping the tool off
  the open web, not protecting secrets) — but it is not security, and
  anyone who views source can read it. If that ever matters, it needs to
  move behind the worker.

- I did not add a "what did the customer say" free-text field on every
  step. It's tempting, but it's a typing task in the middle of a
  conversation. Call notes stay where they were: one box, at the end.

- I did not build call recording, dispositions beyond the existing seven,
  or any kind of queue/CRM view. Those are a different tool.
