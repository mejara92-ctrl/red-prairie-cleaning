No cleaner can guarantee your deposit, but cleaning still matters.

Security deposits can be affected by damage, unpaid rent, trash, carpet issues, missing items, pet problems, and lease terms. But cleaning is one of the most visible things during a final walkthrough, and it is one of the few things you can control before returning keys.

1

Read your move-out instructions first

Before cleaning anything, check the lease, tenant portal, property manager email, or move-out packet. Some offices care about specific items like ovens, carpets, blinds, light fixtures, or fridge drawers.

2

Remove everything before the final clean

Empty homes clean better. Furniture, boxes, trash bags, and leftover items block baseboards, closets, floors, cabinets, and corners.

3

Focus on kitchens and bathrooms

Grease, food residue, hard-water buildup, soap scum, toilets, tubs, sinks, and appliance interiors are high-visibility areas during inspections.

4

Document the final condition

Take photos after cleaning and before turning in keys. Good documentation helps you remember what the property looked like at move-out.

Military moves often involve tight timelines, travel, clearing housing, family logistics, and last-minute inspection windows. If you are leaving Fort Sill or moving into the Lawton area, try to schedule cleaning after the home is empty but before keys are due.

Security deposit cleaning questions

Does professional cleaning guarantee I get my security deposit back?

No. A professional clean can reduce cleaning-related issues, but deposit decisions are made by the landlord or property manager. Damage, unpaid rent, trash, pet issues, and lease terms are separate from cleaning.

Should I clean carpets before returning keys?

Check your lease and move-out instructions. Some properties require professional carpet cleaning, especially for pets or heavy traffic. Red Prairie Cleaning offers carpet extraction cleaning as an add-on.

Is move-out cleaning different from deep cleaning?

Yes. Move-out cleaning is focused on empty-home turnover, inspections, cabinets, appliances, baseboards, floors, bathrooms, and key-return condition. Deep cleaning is usually for lived-in homes that need extra detail.
